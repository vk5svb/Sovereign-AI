Sovereign AI · A brief for decision-makers

Bring the AI to the data.

Every institution will use AI. The most valuable data an institution holds is the data it can never hand to someone else's servers. So the AI must come to the data: on your premises, under your law, reasoning from your values.

Why hosted AI is the wrong place

Three things you cannot outsource

A hosted foreign AI service is brilliant for public work. For confidential work it fails three tests that no contract can fix.

I

The off-switch

Access rests on one company and one country's rules. In June–July 2026, access to two of the newest US frontier models was suspended for about three weeks under US export controls. The customers changed nothing. The rules changed.

Reported · 2026
II

The reach of foreign law

The US CLOUD Act (2018) lets US authorities compel US providers to produce data even when it is stored abroad. In 2020 the EU's top court (Schrems II) struck down the EU–US Privacy Shield over US surveillance access. China's 2017 National Intelligence Law raises mirror-image concerns. To every state, someone else's cloud is a foreign cloud.

Public law
III

The black box

Closed models cannot be inspected by courts, regulators or auditors: what they learned, how they were aligned, what they keep. An on-premise open model, with its sources, rules and audit trail, can be examined line by line.

Audit

Hosted AI: every prompt, file and answer travels to servers in another jurisdiction, where the provider's rules, the host country's laws and the provider's off-switch apply.

The value layer

Same architecture. Each nation's own values.

A general-purpose model reasons mostly from the data it was trained on, and that data is mostly western. A sovereign stack lets each civilization load its own sources, so a judge, a diplomat or a regulator gets reasoning that is recognisably theirs.

An honest caveat

Large hosted models still lead in breadth and raw scale, so use them for public, non-sensitive work. A sovereign stack wins where a nation or firm needs control, trust and low cost in a domain. Gartner projects that over half of enterprise AI models will be domain-specific by 2027, up from about 1% in 2023.

What you stand up

Start with one office. Scale to a nation.

Built from open models and an Indian-built on-premise appliance, designed so that every component can be owned, inspected and moved.

Own it

Server, model weights, data and values layer all belong to the institution. No per-query meter. No outside off-switch.

Prove it

Cited sources, a written rule graph, an audit trail and published benchmarks for accuracy, fidelity to law and honest disagreement.

Start small

One office, one domain, beginning with law and justice. A working prototype is to be demonstrated to national and international dignitaries in January 2027.

Stay open

Open ecosystem, no vendor lock-in: BharatGen or Sarvam in India, other open-weight models elsewhere.

Stay air-gapped

The server is air-gap capable, so the most sensitive workloads need no network path out at all.

Share the blueprint

Global South partners adopt the open components and keep their own data, their own values and their own sovereignty.
